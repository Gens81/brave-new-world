				3. UNDISCLOSED CHANGES




---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

	Persistent BGM		Although the feature of having areas with unique BGM persist throughout
	--------------		random encounters is fairly standard in modern FF6 hacks, it was still a
	 (BTB)			fairly novel concept when we first did it. Below is a list of all of the
				areas in the game where this change applies:

				      •	Narshe Mines		      •	Phantom Forest
				      • Soul Train    		      •	South Figaro - Basement
				      •	Zozo			      •	Vector/Imperial Base
				      •	MagiTek Factory (IMTRF)	      •	Cave To The Sealed Gate
				      •	Thamasa - Burning House	      •	The Floating Continent
				      •	Daryl's Tomb		      •	Narshe (WoR)
				      •	Owzer's House		      •	Phoenix Cave
				      •	Umaro's Cave		      •	Gogo's Cave
				      •	Cyan's Nightmare	      •	The Ancient Castle
				      •	Fanatics Tower		      •	Kefka's Tower

				(Note that the above list excludes areas that already did this, such as
				the Serpent Trench, mine cart ride, and the collapsing house at Tzen.)

	   Doomed World		Further, the "Doomed World" theme now continues to play on the overworld
	   -(Poco Loco)		in the WoR after obtaining the Falcon instead of "Searching For Friends"
				persisting even when you're not in the air.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

	Save & Restore		As mentioned in the readme, several save points have been added to areas
	--------------		that we felt needed them. We have added ten of them in total:
	 (Synchysi)
					• Zozo
					• Cave to the Sealed Gate (treasure room)
					• Burning House
					• Figaro Castle Basement
					• Cave on the Veldt (room after trapped chest)
					• Umaro's Cave
					• Gogo's Cave
					• Ancient Castle(*)
					• Fanatics Tower (last treasure room)
					• Kefka's Tower (4-ton weight room, just after Kaiser)

					*We have no idea why this save point is the wrong color

				Furthermore, several save points have been upgraded to "restore" points
				that will heal your party completely. These tend to be located either in
				areas where the player is "stuck" with no access to a shop and/or rest,
				or occasionally just in really large areas where we felt it appropriate
				to give the player a break. The list of restore points is as follows:

					• Narshe, New Mines
					• Mt. Koltz
					• 1337 River (2nd)
					• South Figaro Basement
					• Soul Train (1st - in caboose)
					• IMTRF (2nd & 3rd - before & after Mine Cart Ride)
					• Burning House(*)
					• Floating Continent (2nd - near Atma)
					• Cyan's Nightmare (1st and 3rd)
					• Kefka's Tower (after Asura/Isis/Myria)

					*This is the "new" save point from above

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

	It Gives You Wings!	Yeah, anyone who saw the name "Red Bull" should not be in the least bit
	-------------------	surprised about their unadvertised side effect. The truth is that Float
	 (BTB)			was actually its original (and only) intended use, and the HP recovery
				was just an afterthought since I'd also been throwing around the idea of
				a slightly better version of Dried Meat that would be useful around the
				time that it starts tapering off and Tonics haven't quite kicked in yet.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

	Settle Down, Beavis	Green Cherries, unlike the above, have had even their primary function
	-------------------	obscured. What players may discover on their own is that they remove the
	 (BTB/Bropedio)		"muddle" and "bserk" statuses (at 50GP less than the cost of a Remedy),
				but what most will not is that they are the only non-lethal way in the
				game to lift the "Rage" and "Dance" statuses (BTB). Further, they will
				also cancel any action that the target has queued (Bropedio)

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

	"Most" Bad Statuses	Regarding the effects of Remedy, it now lifts "most" negative statuses,
	-------------------	which includes Slow and Stop, but not Zombie, Condemned, Sap, or Frozen.
	 (BTB)			Anything that lifts ALL bad statuses (i.e. Starlet or Tapir) will also
				lift those last four. Note that Snake Oil (see "Treasure Hunting" below)
				falls under the latter category, making it very useful in certain cases.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

	Holy Water Cures Sap	I had to do this one as a last-minute change to prevent the Myria fight
	--------------------	from being retardedly hard, since revived allies would otherwise bleed
	 (BTB)			right back to (un)death. As such, this isn't really an intended feature
				of the product nor is it advertised as one.

				As an aside, an interesting bit of trivia about zombified characters is
				that they have a 1/16 chance to inflict Blind and another 1/16 chance to
				inflict Poison with every attack they make. This is actually something
				left over from the original game and not just a larf that we added.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

	No Free Tools		In the original game, Edgar slips an Autocrossbow into your bag when you
	-------------		meet him, making buying one rather pointless. He no longer does this.
	 (Synchysi)
				More imporantly...

	   Mega Autocrossbow	A Figaro castle guard named Julian will mention in the World of Balance
				that he aspires to be a master engineer one day. If you talk to him in
				the World of Ruin with Edgar in your party (the Chancellor will hint to
				this if you talk to him with Edgar in the party), he will give you the
				"schematics" key item which applies the "gauntlet" damage bonus to the
				Autocrossbow, effectively raising its power from 180 to 270, and raises
				its accuracy to 255% (it will not miss unless Edgar/Gogo is blind).

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

	Banon's Lucky Charms	Instead of a Gauntlet or a Genji Glove (neither of which exist anymore),
	--------------------	you now receive a Safety Glove from Banon if you agree to be his "last
	 (Synchysi)		ray of hope" or a Barrier Cube if you deny him three times.

				As for the relics that Banon wears himself, one is a codpiece which acts
				to protect him from seizures (i.e "Sap") that would otherwise murder him
				due to his low HP max combined with the fact that the Albatrosses on the
				1337 River spam Fireball like it's going out of style. As explained in
				the readme (see "Sap/Slow Immunity"), this causes Banon to be immune to
				the "Regen" status as a result. This is largely moot, however, since you
				have no access to Regen at that point outside of a hidden item.

				Banon's OTHER relic - a Talisman - is not there for difficulty concerns,
				but due to a bug wherein leaving him poisoned at the end of his scenario
				would cause the entire Esperville sequence to play out in blur-o-vision
				(this bug does not exist in vanilla since it's impossible for Banon to
				ever become poisoned) due to the fact that Banon and Maduin share a slot
				in the character data and the code doesn't bother doing a status wipe on
				it until later on when it becomes General Compost (I mean Leo).

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

	You Shall Not Pass!	Brave New World removes the two sequence breaks from Locke's scenario by
	-------------------	flagging the hidden tiles behind the pub's as impassable and by telling
	 (Synchysi)		the guard patrolling the edge of town stand the fuck still.

				(It's probably worth mentioning that even if you do still somehow find a
				way to fuck the mod and skip getting Celes, the game will be unbeatable
				since you will be unable to escape the Floating Continent with Kutan.)

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

	Flowers For Doma	As many FF6 fans know, Cyan's original Japanese name is "Cayenne". Since
	----------------	this exceeded the character limitations in English, Ted Woolsey opted to
	(Bropedio/Bauglir)	change his name to something equally stupid. This change inspired us to
				expand the theme naming to include all of the citizens of Doma, but we
				quickly realized that female names in this vein are far more common than
				masculine ones. As a result, Cyan's son is now a daughter named Violet.

				One interesting issue we ran into with this gender swap is the fact that
				the "dead" little boy sprite used when Cyan discovers his dead family is
				completely unique to that scene and the counterpart for the girl sprite
				is this "shocked/wide-eyed" expression that ended up being some unholy
				combination of hilarious and creepy when we tried to use it as a corpse.
				Ultimately, Bauglir was commissioned to edit the original "dead" sprite
				to look like a girl (his work can be seen in the "Gallery" folder).

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

	Sales To Die For	Merchant ghosts on the Soul Train are now much easier to identify due to
	----------------	their coloring. We've also added one of these guys to the final segment
	 (Synchysi)		of Cyan's Nightmare since you're likely running low on supplies at that
				point and you're kinda stuck there until you can kill Wrexsoul.

	   Braaaaaains...	The Ghost Ring (AKA "Relic Ring") now grants ALL of the advantages and
	   -(BTB)		disadvantages of being a walking corpse. This includes immunity to all
				statuses but "slow", "sap", and death, as well as the standard undead
				fire/holy weaknesses paired with the ability to absorb dark damage.

				Of course, the Ghost Ring also does the whole "undead reversal" thing
				with attacks and other abilities that cure or absorb HP. The list of
				everything that will NOT reverse on the undead is as follows:

						• All items (except for Phoenix Down/Tear)
						• Life/Life 2(*)
						• Mana Battery
						• Mantra & Chakra
						• Go Fish/Jackpot
						• Bedevil
						• Harvester
						• Paintbrushes/Healing Shiv

					*This is to prevent them from insta-killing undead bosses

				Despite this, the major downside of the Relic Ring in the original game
				wasn't difficulty healing the wearer, but rather that they couldn't be
				revived in battle by any means if they died. This issue is addressed in
				Brave New World by applying the "Overcast" effect to the Ghost Ring so
				that users become zombies at 0 HP (which can be cured with Holy Water)
				rather than dying outright - assuming, of course, that the player isn't
				stupid enough to pair the Ghost Ring with instant death protection.

	   Zombie-D		However, perhaps the oddest case of undead fuckery is the White Dragon.
	   -(BTB)		The main reason behind giving the Remedy and Regen(X) spells one-sided
				targeting was not to prevent any sort of abuse as with the Life spells,
				but rather to mask the fact that they lack the Holy element that makes
				Cure magic so deliciously effective against the undead. This, in turn,
				was the reason that we got rid of equipment and enemies that block or
				absorb holy damage. The White-D was a necessary holdout since all of the
				dragons need to absorb their own element. This then meant that Holy Wind
				(which also has the "Holy" elemental flag) would pull a reversal and end
				up hurting it, instead. Making the White Dragon undead triggers a DOUBLE
				reversal for Holy Wind, causing everything to work out in the end.

	Party Of One		The Battle of Narshe is now a two-party campaign instead of three since

				standing next to Banon) do nothing.

	...SOLD!		The Auction House in Jidoor has been permanently shut down and replaced
	--------		with the "advanced" Beginner's School. This allows the one in Narshe to
	(Synchysi)		focus on general changes without overwhelming new players with advanced

				Regarding the magicite pieces that were once sold at the Auction House,
				they can now be purchased from "Bob", the crazy asshole who lives north
				of Kohlingen and aspires to build a Colosseum. If you haven't purchased
				both before reaching the WoR, you can buy them from him at the Colosseum
				(and get an idea of just how badly he fucked himself over to build it in
				the process). Either way, it's a huge improvement over sitting through
				long, unskippable cutscenes at the Auction House over and over and over
				again until the RNG finally stopped being a bag of shitty fuckness.

				As for the origin of the name "Bob", this is actually a good example of
				how Synchysi and I work. When we first set out to axe the Auction House
				and decided where to move Golem and Zoneseek, Synchysi gave me a general
				outline of dialogue that I needed to write for the new events using the
				standard "Alice and Bob" example format and...well, the rest is history.
				Another example is the time I pointed to an enemy sprite, asked him WTF
				it was supposed to be, and he just shrugged and said, "cave stuff".

	Heaven Can Wait		"Go to the Floating Continent" (when available) is no longer the default

	Go Fish			In the original game, fish labeled as "rotten" were actually those that

					• Rotten Fish (previously "Fish"): -16

	The Wolf & The Bird	An astute veteran player might look at the new esper restrictions in the
	-------------------	printme and note that, since Fenrir is only usable by Shadow and Gau,
	 (Synchysi)		there is absolutely no point whatsoever in heading over to Mobliz until

				locations of Fenrir and Palidor so that going to Mobliz early will give

	   Phunbaba Returns	...if, on the other hand, you don't go to Mobliz until after getting the

				soon as you leave the one where everybody lives.

	Tentacle Grape		The reworked battle script for the Tentacle battle assumes a party that
	--------------		includes Sabin. Because of this, it was necessary to call an alternate
	(Synchysi/BTB)		formation for wackjobs who fail to recruit him so that they don't end
				up with a b0rked tentacle battle.

				So, how does the Tentacle battle work? Well, originally, quite shittily.
				In order to prevent the Tentacles from grabbing a character that was
				already being held, they were scripted to only Grab characters afflicted
				with the "slow" status, which the Grab attack then lifted. Smart players
				quickly realized that this entire battle could be shut down by equipping
				Running Shoes beforehand, as the perma-haste would prevent any unwanted
				Grabbing from occurring (also very useful on the subways in New York).

				In Brave New World, each Tentacle is scripted to Grab only a specific
				character, with the fourth one (bottom left) scripted to take over for
				any Tentacles that have been killed. In the alternate formation that's
				called if Sabin is not present, his Tentacle (upper right) simply no
				longer tries to Grab anything. This does make the fight somewhat easier
				if you know which Tentacles to prioritize killing off, but still much
				harder overall without Sabin (this isn't vanilla, after all).

	DG-GPS			Because hunting for Doom Gaze is a huge pain in the ass, we've added an
	------			option on the Falcon's helm that finds him for you. Note that you still
	(Synchysi)		can run into him the regular way, so it's a still bad idea to have Relm
				piloting the ship alone and naked (at least until you kill Doom Gaze).

				FUN RANDOM FACT: the flight path that the Falcon takes when you look for

	Cross & Blast		The Mind Blast and N.Cross attacks both have complex targeting effects
	-------------		which are heavily modified in Brave New World. This entry is mostly just
	(Seibaby/Bropedio)	an excuse to credit Seibaby and Bropedio for their work on them (because
				God knows these two names don't show up enough in my documentation), but
				it's also worth getting into the details for anyone interested.

				First, Mind Blast. To the external observer, it would appear to follow
				the same rules as Cyan's 4-hit Bushidos, making four hits with each one
				running its own evasion check. What it does instead in the original game
				is check evasion for each target first and then hit four times at random
				in the "pool" of everyone who didn't dodge. The result is, statistically
				speaking, a less even distribution of hits throughout the party, but a
				guarantee that all four hits will land unless every character manages to
				avoid the attack (unlikely). Especially bearing in mind the latter, this
				approach diminishes the value of evasion (or stamina in Brave New World)
				to mitigate problems. Thus, Mind Blast is changed here to simply attack
				five times (instead of four), checking against stamina for each hit.

				N.Cross, by comparison, was much simpler (if not much stupider) in the
				original game. Its special effect simply ran a coin flip as a secondary
				layer of evasion from the attack, ostensibly as an attempt to prevent an
				unlucky roll from freezing your entire party. Of course, bad rolls still
				happened and even moderate luck meant that nobody would get hit at all.
				This is a case where the "Mind Blast" approach would have worked much
				better, just using two hits instead of four, which is exactly what we've
				done here. Stamina remains important insomuch that it helps lower the
				pool size, thus increasing the odds of only one character getting hit,
				but the likelihood of it entirely missing a party of four is very low.

				 (NOTE: Green-D's "Thriller" attack uses the same targeting as N.Cross)

	Veldt Encounters	As the readme mentioned, great effort has gone into making rage hunting
	----------------	as painless of an experience as possible. For the curious and/or bored,
	 (BTB)			this section will detail the extent of those efforts. As you know, there
				are now only 64 rages instead of 255, enemies without rages won't appear
				on the Veldt, and enemies WITH rages can be leapt on even outside of the
				Veldt. That last bit might indicate to the casual reader that the Veldt
				is basically a waste of time since it's now easier to hunt for rages at
				their source, but a complete overhaul of the enemy encounter structure
				aims to make the Veldt the easiest place to catch 'em all.

				First, a quick glossary of terms:

					Formation - any enemy encounter, random or otherwise

					Pack - a list of enemy formations and their odds of appearing

					Chunk - what the Veldt uses instead of normal packs

				Got it? Good.

				Simply put, the Veldt utilizes a special enemy pack that cycles through
				the game's first 512 enemy formations in chunks of 8. That is, the first
				encounter randomly selects between formations 0 and 7, the next one will
				pick between 8 and 15, then 16 to 23, and so on. Have you ever wondered
				why encounters on the Veldt always seemed to start with Leafers and then
				get progressively harder until they reset? Well, this is why. One might
				say that they tried to mask this fact by throwing a handful of late-game
				encounters in the lower formations, but a cursory glance at the original
				code suggests that little, if any of that fucking mess was intentional.

				Part of the original game's problem should already be apparent: you had
				a 1/8 chance of encountering many foes on the Veldt, and even then only
				on the one chunk of the cycle where their formation fell. Anyone with no
				knowledge of how this system worked could easily waste months of their
				lives in pursuit of elusive rages that, tragically, were about 90% shit.

				It's important to note that all formations in a chunk (usually referred
				to as packs elsewhere, but I call them chunks here to avoid confusing
				them with regular packs) do not have equal chances of being selected
				because of how the selection process works. First, the game finds the
				first chunk that contains at least one Veldt-enabled formation that's
				been "unlocked" by being encountered. It then selects one of the eight
				slots in that chunk at random. If the selected slot is not Veldt-enabled
				or unlocked, then it will move forward, looping back around to the start
				if it reaches the end, until it finds one that is.

				As an example, take formations 16-23. Assuming that both formations have
				been unlocked, the Scrapper/2x Vaporite has a 3/4 chance of being called
				while the 2x Scrapper formation has only 1/4. This is because if any of
				the empty slots are chosen, it skips ahead to the first one that isn't.

				Thus, not only is the headache of rage hunting alleviated by the overall
				reduction of rages to find, but also by the rearrangement of formations
				that makes them more likely to be encountered along with a lower number
				of chunks in total that must be cycled through (64 in vanilla, 25 here)
				in order to get another shot at encountering them.

				But wait, there's more! Did you miss your chance to unlock a formation
				from an area of the game that you can't go back to, like the Soul Train
				or the IMTRF? Well, first of all, I tried to work the encounter rates so
				that this sort of thing is very unlikely to occur. But on the off chance
				it happens, you can now find all of the missable enemy formations in the
				WoB in either the "Grenade Forest" west of the Veldt (for the Soul Train
				enemies) or on Triangle Island (for everything else). For the encounters
				from the Floating Continent, look just south of Albrook in the WoR.

				As an interesting side note, since these formations are from areas with
				persistent background music (see far above), encountering them will not
				interrupt the overworld BGM, either. It's actually quite eerie.

	Limit Breaks		Something that's discussed in the readme is that desperation attacks
	------------		(i.e. primitive limit breaks) now do damage based on the user's stamina
	 (BTB)			rather than their magic power. I feel like this makes a lot more sense,
				plus it doesn't result in characters like Relm and Strago always doing
				more damage with them than, say, Cyan. But remember that stamina attacks
				are still technically magical, and so they're unaffected by relics that
				boost physical damage, as well as the back-row damage penalty.

				All desperation attacks now also have the following properties:

						• Power = 150*
						• Ignores defense/row
						• Sets Sap

					*Except Gogo's, which just does 9,999 damage

				How do desperation attacks work? Well, I'm glad you asked. As has always
				been the case, any character in the "near fatal" status (HP < 1/8 MaxHP)
				has a 1/16 chance of performing a desperation attack in lieu of a normal
				one if "Fight" is used. Desperation attacks tend to show up a lot more
				often in Brave New World due to a combination of regular attacks being
				more useful on the whole and a MUCH greater likelihood of its characters
				being in the "near fatal" status. However, there are two conditions that
				will prevent desperation attacks from occurring:

				   ...if the character has "image", "vanish", "muddle", or "zombie"

				   ...if the character has already used a DA in this fight

				There was a third condition in vanilla where the global battle timer had
				to have at least reached 768 (about 25 seconds of real time), but that
				condition was removed by the nATB system.

				(Oh, and Gau and Umaro don't have desperation attacks because they don't
				have a "Fight" command. But I'm sure you knew that already.)

	N.O. E.S.C.A.P.E.	There are a few anomalies (one might call them "bugs") with how running
	-----------------	from fights works in the game. First of all, inability to run is a flag
	 (BTB)			on individual enemies rather than on the formation itself. The result is
				that it's possible in the original game to run away from the Ifrit/Shiva
				fight during the brief period when there's technically no enemies on the
				battlefield, which of course isn't possible now for obvious reasons.

				(NOTE FOR DEVELOPERS: there is a formation flag to prevent running, but
				it's very buggy and should not, under any circumstances, be used.)

				The other snag is that I wanted to avoid certain random encounters being
				inescapable because some or all of the enemies in it can be encountered
				elsewhere in fights that you shouldn't be able to run from, and so I've
				just said "fuck it" and enabled running away from those enemies. The big
				examples here are enemies from "event" battles that later appear on the
				Veldt (see above), namely the Soldier/Tek Armor battles in the Imperial
				Camp and two of the three enemies (Albatross and Exocite) that appear on
				the 1337 River. The latter creates an interesting scenario where you can
				run from any fight there so long as you kill off any Nautiloids first.

				There's also the soldiers you talk to before the Imperial Banquet, which
				I could very easily fix just by making those fights with unique enemies,
				but I honestly really don't give enough of a shit. You want to run from
				the soldiers at Vector? Be my fucking guest.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

				GENERAL TRANSLATION NOTES
				-------------------------

One of the initial primary goals of our re-translation efforts was to avoid the game having an "obvious
fan translation" feel about it. When we started getting a bit more reference-happy (note the size of the
above section), that kinda went out the window. Likely the biggest turning point in that regard was when
we did the Phantom/Soul Train sequence and realized that we had made it into a complete joke. Of course,
it was a complete joke in the original game, as well, and so the goal then became - as Synchysi put it -
to maintain the atmosphere of every scene. Thus, the silly scenes remain silly in Brave New World while
the serious ones are treated with respect - and hopefully everything feels polished and not halfassed.

Our writing aims to strike a balance between what actually worked in the Ted Woolsey translation, the
original Japanese, and a healthy dose of our own creative efforts. To paraphrase a review written by one
of our early adapters - we aimed more to take what was already there and build on it in a meaningful way
that celebrates what made the original game great rather than trying to change the game into something
else entirely. Yes, there are a lot of wacky, off-the-wall lines here that serve as reminders that you
are NOT playing the original game, but none of them, we feel, should be a detriment to the main story.
As a general rule, the more lemony something is, the more you'll have to go out of your way to find it.

A rather notable exception to the above rule is Narshe. You see, a disproportionately large amount of
Final Fantasy VI's fanbase - and, by extention, Brave New World's fanbase - hail from the Great White
North. It's David Hasselhoff in Germany kind of thing - floppyheads love them some FF6. In response to
this, the people of Narshe were written as my loiving shout-out to my friends up north

(And yes, I will readily admit to learning everything I know about Canada from South Park.)

A major impetus of mine was to clarify certain plot elements that weren't adequately explained. A great
deal of new dialogue was added, for example, to clarify the logistics of Sabin's scenario - namely the
fact that he spends most of it traveling in the opposite direction of where he intends to go - or, to a
lesser extent, the logic behind escaping to Narshe via the 1337 River in the first place. Many lines in
the World of Ruin were similarly added or rewritten to help direct the player to the appropriate places,
with particular attention given to the needlessly cryptic subplot about finding the Phoenix Cave.

Another thing that I took every possible effort to do was minimize the amount of unassigned lines in the
game. Unassigned lines are spoken by unspecified characters in your party in situations where there are
no mandatory characters and the game can't know who will or will not be present. This is most prevalent
in the World of Ruin, which is largely open-ended. Addressing the issue involved rewriting the dialogue
in the character re-recruitment scenes to make the character being recruited do most/all of the talking;
unassigned lines that were necessary to keep the monologue going were kept as laconic as possible.

Censorship is something else that I should touch on, going back to the above point regarding the writing
being based on several different versions of the game. I'll go into a bit more detail about the worst
offenders in the character section below, but the long and the short of it is that the American version,
being a Nintendo game in the 90's, was very obviously censored. We thus aimed to remove that censorship,
as we felt that it served to the detriment of the game to have situations wherein people did not talk in
a realistic fashion. It wasn't until we sat down to address the single biggest complaint about earlier
versions of the mod - the level of swearing that it contained - that we truly realized exactly how much
swearing there was in this Goddamn motherfucking cunt-ass bag of shitdicks. And so we toned it down.

In addition to cutting back on the amount of swearing in the mod, we also introduced the optional Clean
New World patch that censored it ENTIRELY. This was done less to appease the vocal minority of players
who seemed to take issue with any degree of swearing whatsoever and more as a personal "thank you" to a
man who contributed a tremendous amount of useful feedback and insight, proclaimed Brave New World to be
a huge improvement to his favorite game in every way, and disliked only that the amount of swearing it
contained rendered it inappropriate to play through with his younger sister. However, unlike the above-
mentioned self-censorship, which we voluntarily implemented as we felt it added much to the story that
had been taken away by liberal amounts of swearing, Clean New World is completely irreverent and silly.

Despite the above, I'm often accused of having written Clean New World to spite our detractors, which is
entirely untrue. What WOULD have been written purely out of spite would be the oft-requested "vanilla"
style script that reverted as much of the dialogue as possible to the way it was in the original game.
While I won't take offense to criticism aimed at Brave New World, I am VERY opposed to the notion that
there was nothing wrong with the original game and that it should be left as-is. Thus, I had a standing
offer to the community as a whole that anyone who so desired to write this "vanilla" translation would
have my resources and support behind them, but I personally would take no active giving of fucks due to
conflict(/lack) of interest. Most people who stepped up to the plate immediately backed down once they
realized the amount of work that any re-translation effort would entail, but one brave soul finally did
persevere and thus "Bland New World" was born.

Finally, another big concern that we had to deal with was space constraints. Not only is there just so
much text you can fit into those caption boxes, there's also a finite amount of space in the data bank
where dialogue is stored. Thus, players may notice that I have employed a large number of space-saving
tricks to stretch my dialogue dollar as far as it can possibly go, such as switching the clock puzzle at
Zozo over to Roman numerals (this wasn't, as some have assumed, my attempt at a Leisure Suit Larry-esque
age verification puzzle). Standardizing the dialogue to no longer specify who's talking unless it's a
named character or if more than one person is speaking also really helped. There's even one case where I
took advantage of the fact that inserting a call for a character's name used less space than typing out
something else would, so there's one line (we'll call it an easter egg) that will look rather weird if a
certain character's name has been changed from the default.

				NOTES ON CHARACTERS
				-------------------

Moving on to our translation choices for individual characters, the duties were split between Synchysi
and myself. Synchysi wrote most of the lines for Kefka, Leo, Banon, Shadow (in his flashbacks), Strago,
Setzer when he's not being a dick, Gau, Gogo, and early-game Celes; I was mostly responsible for Sabin,
Setzer when he *is* being a dick, Locke, Relm, Mog, Umaro, late-game Celes, and the bulk of the game's
NPC's. I forget which of us worked on Cyan, especially since Bishop (shout-outs) came along and revised
his dialogue into proper "Ye Olde Broken English", Edgar... really hasn't changed much at all, and Terra
is still the same flat, boring character that she's always been to a satirical degree. Synchysi's edits,
particularly for Celes and Leo, were largely based on Lina Darkstar's translation of the original script
and aim to provide a more faithful presentation of the game's original characterization whereas I took a
bit more in the way of creative liberties with my edits.

To start, the duality between Terra and Celes is heavily played up in Brave New World. Both were granted
"main character" status as far as game mechanics were concerned (hence their above-average versatility)
and their available spells and equipment contrast wherever they're not mirrored, such as the case with
the Apocyllumina and Gem/Soul Box or how Terra learns Shell (as she's the more magic-focused of the two)
while Celes (the more well-balanced one) instead gets Safe. This duality extends to their personalities,
as well: Celes is a dynamic, multi-layered character who grows and develops significantly throughout the
course of the game, whereas Terra simply has no personality whatsoever.

As I mentioned, Celes's early-game personality is largely based on Lina Darkstar's interpretation of the
original Japanese script. She is very formal and blunt, much to the point that she displays a perceived
lack of femininity. The English language lacks much capacity to convey tone of speech via text, which is
a significant source of things like this that tend to get lost in translation from languages that suffer
no such drawbacks (namely Japanese). Whereas Woolsey portrays Celes as only marginally more progressive
than the traditional "damsel in distress" character that's mostly represented in FF6 by Terra, Synchysi
introduces her as a strong, confident, and also a bit stubborn character whose barriers only begin to
come down when she takes the stage at the opera house with far less hesitation and much more excitement
than she would have her travel companions believe.

The end of the World of Balance is about the point where I mostly took over Celes's lines, and my goals
were twofold. First, I had to continue writing her as the strong character that Synchysi had set her up
to be as opposed to the bland, love-starved twat she originally became in the World of Ruin. Second, I
wanted to make her actually feel conflicted in her loyalties (something that the original game clearly
tried to do, but failed miserably at) so that the scene on the Floating Continent where she's forced to
choose a side held some real dramatic weight. From both of these objectives was born the mod's central
theme of Celes's character, which is her upbringing as a cold, emotionless killer with no place in the
world other than what she has been bred to do. It is only through coming to terms with this that she's
able to embrace her feelings for Locke and discover her true purpose in life.

A major point of debate regarding Celes in Brave New World is the little hissy fit/emo breakdown she had
in its earlier versions during the Nimufu fight at the end of the Floating Continent, which I have since
rewritten because even I thought it was narmy as hell. This does shift the focus of the scene a bit away
from the feelings of being nothing more than an instrument of war and more toward the insinuation that
her powers are rooted in the powers of darkness rather than light, a fact which is further alluded to by
the fact that Shock now drains its user's HP (à la Cecil's "Dark Wave" ability in Final Fantasy IV). But
although this scene was a central point of inspiration for the other choices I made for Celes, the truth
is that it exists solely for balance reasons rather than for story purposes. Because Celes has to solo
the next bit of the game, Nimufu will give her 10,000 experience if she is under level 18; the in-battle
approach (as opposed to just re-averaging her level) is an artifact left over from before we separated
esper stat gains from the regular leveling system.

Moving on, we have Kefka. I should probably start off by saying that American Kefka and Japanese Kefka
are two VERY different characters, and the American version is largely considered to be one of Woolsey's
finer accomplishments. That said, he was still very much a product of censorship, which we aimed to get
rid of while still retaining Woolsey's unique spin on him. Kefka is one of two characters in Brave New
World - the other being Relm - who was initially given carte blanche on the swearing front, and oh, did
he swear. The problem is that, rather than the intended effect of making Kefka come across as childish,
immature, and juvenile, it made *us* come across as childish, immature, and juvenile.

As I mentioned above, the biggest complaint we received about earlier versions of the mod was the level
of swearing it had, and the vast majority of those complaints were aimed at Kefka. In response to this,
we overhauled Kefka's character to emphasize his... effeminate nature. He remains composed and actually
quite a bit of a dick (at least whenever he's not being a transparent douche to General Leo) when things
are going his way, but he quickly loses his shit and starts acting like a two year-old when they aren't.
In either case, his mannerisms and word choices point to someone who's more than just a little bit of a
gigantic fucking fairy, which is why it was so easy for Woolsey to re-imagine him as a clown. While he
still does swear in current versions, we made an active decision to reserve the first instance of it to
the scene where Celes stabs him at the Floating Continent so that it would have maximum impact.

Moving on to the characters that I wrote for, Sabin was by far one of my favorites. What I particularly
like about him is just how close his resulting characterization is to the original, yet how much more of
a personality he seems to have at the same time. As I mentioned earlier, BNW's Sabin is based wholesale
on Rei of BoF3 fame, and it's interesting how little I had to alter Sabin's lines to make the two match
up, given that Rei is a thief whose dialogue was clearly written by a bogan and Sabin is a monk of royal
heritage. The common bond between the two is that both characters exist outside of common society, and
their actions often tend to convey a lack of familiarity with social graces. In Sabin's case, however,
it's unclear just how much of his behavior can be attributed to his lack of social contact and how much
of it is due to both his upbringing and training as a monk affording him a great deal of confidence in
himself (one might say TOO much confidence) along with a certain degree of aloofness.

With Cyan, the initial goal was merely to make his speech patterns more consistent. In the original, he
dropped his Ye Olde Butchered English about a third of the way through the game and just started talking
like a normal (albeit a very bland) person. From there, it was only a short jump to mocking him at every
given opportunity since the way he talks is actually really stupid (note that, even in his home country,
nobody ELSE talks like that, making him much like Frog from Chrono Trigger in that regard). Synchysi and
I throughly enjoyed the contrast of by far the game's darkest character becoming a complete walking joke
who won't shut the fuck up about being the retainer to the dead king of an uninhabited country. We even
took a shot at Doma itself by giving its citizens more colorful names - personally, I consider it a lost
opportunity that the king's name isn't "Crimson".

(Also, changing Cyan's son to a daughter wasn't me being progressive or anything. It's just a lot easier
to come up with female names that are also colors than male ones.)

As mentioned above, we had some external help from a fan for ensuring that all of Cyan's dialogue was as
accurate as possible to the "ye olden" theme. One moment that stands out to me in particular was looking
over his (extremely in-depth) notes and seeing him metion the word "horarium" to refer to the train's
schedules, but suggested that I not use it because nobody would know what the hell I was talking about.
My response was, "no, no... I can work with this," and thus one of my favorite lines in BNW was born.

Next up is Setzer, who's another one of those characters that's very different depending on the version.
He was a much darker character in the original Japanese who obviously never got over the death of Daryl,
whereas Woolsey re-envisioned him as a more carefree, happy-go-lucky kinda guy since suicidal depression
didn't really go over too well with 90's-era Nintendo of America. When writing Setzer, I chose to focus
on the one trait common to both versions - that he's kind of a prick - and dial it up to 11. Setzer is
now thus something of a grade-A asshole, and I leave it up to the player to decide exactly how much of
that is because of Daryl. As a result, his motives for joining up with the Returners now fall somewhere
between the original's "I have nothing to live for," and Woolsey's "Meh, I'm just bored".

(For the record, the heavily-modified scene at Kohlingen with Celes re-recruiting Setzer is my favorite
one in the mod. Thanks to Kain for inspiring me to re-write it from the original.)

While Relm did get toned down a bit along with Kefka back when we realized that there was far much more
swearing in this mod than we'd originally thought, she's still the undisputed champion of the category.
Granted, our aim was to make her more offensive than vulgar, but if Kick-Ass has taught us anything it's
that it's hilarious when a prepubescent girl swears a lot and murders people. This is why I pretty much
wrote her as a Hit Girl clone, since that's kind of her entire point.

One thing we did want to drive home, however, is that Relm does care for Strago in spite of how much she
rags on him. Her constant reference to him as a doddering old coot is meant to be affectionate, and it's
actually quite accurate. Strago is written as a good-natured goofball who mostly serves as a long-winded
Mr. Exposition whenever he's given lines, generally droning on and on until someone else shuts him up.
One might speculate that Relm is the way she is due to a general lack of discipline from her laid-back
grandfather, and that Strago in turn is one of the few people actually willing to tolerate her shit.

As for everyone else... Locke is still a large ham with a severe white knight complex and the occasional
tendency to ruin moments by shoving his foot in his mouth; Gau's speech patterns are now more deliberate
and less caveman-like; Mog was rewritten as an obvious author avatar given his "blatant corporate mascot
insertion" status in the original; Shadow was one of the earliest victims of the dialogue space crunch,
which is why half of his lines were rewritten to just "..."; and I swear that Gogo is channeling some
unholy trinity of Christopher Walken, William Shatner, and Nicholas Cage.

Oh, yeah, and one last thing regards two minor characters in the game who in the original game were both
named "Impresario". The first is the conductor of the Soul Train, and I really have no fucking clue why
he's named Impresario in the first place. He's renamed in the mod to Charon, after the ferryman of the
River Styx who's made appearances in other Squaresoft games in the same role. The second is the *actual*
Impresario of the opera house, whose real name in Brave New World is revealed to be Dancho. I did this
because Dancho is his actual name in the original Japanese; the FakeMustache relic (not present in the
mod since the Control ability has been removed) was originally also named after him for some reason.

				ITEM AND ENEMY NAMES
				--------------------

My primary goal with renaming enemies, attacks, and items was eliminating all instances of truncated and
CamelCased names by coming up with ones that fit within the allotted space. It was difficult, given the
extremely small character limits I had to work with (10 characters for enemy names and attacks, 12 for
items and equipment) and led to some of my more interesting choices. The one that comes immediately to
mind is "kite", which I use because it's the smallest word I could find that refers to a type of shield.
There are also a lot of people who apparently have never heard the word "bolo" before (it's a sword).
We did cheat the character limitation at exactly one point, however, with the Defibrillator. This was
accomplished by taking two unused character tiles and turning them into "ill", which is why they appear
closer together in that name than they otherwise would.

The original game was also chock full of stupid names, damn near to the point that I don't know what Ted
Woolsey was smoking when he came up with half of it. Beyond renaming things to work within the game's
space constraints, I also set out to rename everything that made me go, "...what," which unfortunately
was most of it. When at all possible, I used their original Japanese names (Zantetsuken, Sakura/Kagenui,
all of Cyan's katanas except the Hanzo) or their correct translations (Life Bell and Heiji's Coin are a
bit derivative, but qualify). Failing that, I would use names that showed up in any other Final Fantasy
games, steal names from non-Final Fantasy games (see the reference section above), or just dig around
through the fantasy kitchen sink until I found something that worked.

Finally, every enemy has a "special" attack (you'll recognize these as what Gau's rages now exclusively
use in place of regular attacks) which is basically a regular attack with either an added status effect
or a damage multiplier. The names of these attacks are set individually for each enemy, and I made it a
specific point to go through and rename them all so that each one was unique (many in the original were
not). Many of the new names are references (again, see the reference section above), some of them are
jokes that you probably won't get unless you compare them to the special attacks used by other enemies
in the same sprite group, but most of them are just funny-sounding words that tested my vocabulary and
thesaurus skills. I may list all of them here one day, but for now I'll just leave it as is.
